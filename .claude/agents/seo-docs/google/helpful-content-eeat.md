# Helpful Content & E-E-A-T

**Source:** https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content
**Last synced:** 2026-08-20

## E-E-A-T (in priority order — Trust is most important)

- **Experience** — first-hand practical knowledge (actually used the product/service)
- **Expertise** — demonstrable knowledge of subject
- **Authoritativeness** — recognized authority in the domain
- **Trustworthiness** — reliable, well-sourced, transparent origins (MOST critical)

Content need not demonstrate all four equally — some rely on experience, others on expertise.

## People-first content markers

**Positive signals:**
- Serves an actual audience directly
- Demonstrates first-hand expertise
- Site has clear focus and purpose
- Reader leaves informed, having achieved their goal
- Visitor experiences satisfaction, not frustration

**Warning signs (search-first content):**
- Content created primarily to attract SERP traffic
- Producing lots of content across unrelated topics hoping some rank
- Mass-produced without expertise
- Rewriting competitor content without adding value
- Targeting trending topics outside expertise area

## "Who, How, Why" test

Google evaluates:

- **WHO created it** — bylines, author bios linking to profile pages, credentials
- **HOW it was created** — disclose automation/AI use; explain why AI added value
- **WHY it was created** — should be to help people, not to manipulate rankings

## Self-assessment questions

- Does it provide original info, insight, or analysis beyond the obvious?
- Substantial descriptions of the topic?
- Free of spelling/factual errors?
- Written or reviewed by demonstrable expert?
- Author credentials clear?
- Would you cite this as a trustworthy source?
- Would you trust financial/medical info in this content?

## YMYL (Your Money or Your Life)

Elevated E-E-A-T scrutiny for topics affecting:
- Health
- Finances
- Safety
- Societal welfare

For YMYL topics: require author credentials, professional review, source citations.

## Anti-patterns (avoid)

- Mass-producing content on many topics without expertise
- Extensive unreviewed AI generation for rank manipulation
- Rewriting competitors without substantial additions
- Artificially altering publication dates to appear "fresh"
- Targeting trending topics outside your expertise
- Writing to hit specific word counts
- Promising answers to questions with no confirmed solutions

## SEO's proper role

SEO is legitimate when applied to people-first content. It becomes manipulation when applied to search-first content. The distinction hinges on motivation.

## E-commerce application

- Show real product experience — original photos, hands-on descriptions, first-person impressions
- Author/reviewer bylines on product content
- Real customer reviews (with `Review` schema)
- Clear About page, contact info, return policy (trust signals)
- Author bios for buying guides / editorial content
- Cite manufacturer specs; add original testing data where possible
